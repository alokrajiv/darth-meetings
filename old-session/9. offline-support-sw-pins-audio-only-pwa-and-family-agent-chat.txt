⏺ Verdict first: yes, this is doable, and the app's shape helps. Everything the
  meeting page shows already arrives through plain GET fetches from a client
  component, so a service worker can answer those same URLs from a local cache
  when the network is gone with almost no page rewiring. One real complication
  came out of the survey.

  The one hard part: "audio" and "video" are the same file. For recorded
  meetings the audio route streams the stored mp4. Audio-only offline needs an
  audio-only derivative, so the server would extract one with ffmpeg on first
  request and keep it next to the original. ffmpeg is already installed on the
  VM and there is 340 GB free there.

  What the numbers say about the defaults you proposed (from the live database
  and storage on the VM today):

  ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────┐
  │                     Item                      │          Size           │
  ├───────────────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┤
  │ Transcript JSON, average per meeting          │ 0.9 MB, max 8.7 MB      │
  ├───────────────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┤
  │ Last 100 meetings, transcript + notes +       │ roughly 100 MB          │
  │ report                                        │                         │
  ├───────────────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┤
  │ Audio-only extract, per hour of meeting       │ roughly 30 MB at 64     │
  │                                               │ kbps                    │
  ├───────────────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┤
  │ Last 10 audio                                 │ roughly 300 MB          │
  ├───────────────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┤
  │ Stored videos                                 │ 20 MB to 520 MB each    │
  └───────────────────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────┘

  Chrome on a laptop handles this easily. Safari evicts site storage after 7
  days of non-use unless the app is installed to the Dock, so I would ship a
  web-app manifest and tell people to install it if they use Safari.

  How it would work

  - Service worker intercepts GET calls under the meeting API paths and the
    static bundle. Online, it passes through and refreshes the cache for pinned
    meetings. Offline, or when the network fails, it serves the cached copy.
    Media Range requests are answered by slicing the cached file, so seeking
    still works.
  - Auto policy runs in the background while online. It pins the newest 100
    meetings for transcript, the newest 10 for audio, and none for video. Items
    roll off as newer ones arrive. Manual pins never roll off.
  - Manual override lives in the meeting page's overflow menu. Choose transcript
    only, plus audio, or plus audio and video. Multi-part recordings pin every
    part.
  - Offline mode is read-only. Reading, playback, click-to-seek, notes, reports,
    and search inside a meeting work. Edits, sharing, AI actions, and imports
    are disabled with a short explanation. The archive shows cached meetings
    normally and greys out the rest.
  - Settings card shows the three defaults as editable numbers, this device's
    storage use, the list of manual pins with their level, and a clear-all
    button. The defaults would be stored per account on the server. Pins and
    cached bytes are per device by nature, and the card would say so.
  - Logout wipes the cache, since cached transcripts sit unencrypted in the
    browser profile.

  Effort is about two thousand lines across a service worker, a pin registry,
  one new server route for the audio extract, the settings card, and the menu. I
  estimate one to two days of focused work including end-to-end testing against
  the deployed app. I have not written any code yet, since the ask ended in a
  question. Say the word and I will start with the service worker and audio
  extract, then the UI.

⏺ What "audio only" means. For a recorded Meet or Teams call, the app stores one
  mp4 that carries both the video track and the audio track, and the player
  streams that same file whether you show the video or not. So "keep the audio

  added that: the first time a meeting is pinned at audio level, ffmpeg extracts

❯ so moving forward there is always an audio is it? btw can audio and video     
  channel be seperated - how do ppl do this well? like if we have audio and     
  video - can the web player be smart and sync and do this                      

  Thought for 20s (ctrl+o to expand)

⏺ Is there always an audio file now? Not yet. The extract is made lazily, the
  first time a meeting is pinned at audio level, so the first pin of a long
  recording waits about a minute per hour of meeting. Making it always exist is
  a small change: run the same ffmpeg step when an import or upload completes,
  in the background. Cost is roughly a minute of CPU per meeting hour and 30 MB
  of disk, and it would also let the normal player offer an "audio only" mode on
  slow connections. I'd do that as the next step if you want it.

  Can audio and video be separated and kept in sync? Yes, and there are three
  ways people do it, from janky to proper.

  - Two elements, JS glue. A muted <video> plus an <audio>, with a timer nudging
    one to match the other. Works in demos, drifts in practice: one element
    buffers while the other plays, seeks land at different keyframes, and mobile
    Safari suspends background elements. Not something I'd ship.
  - One file, two tracks, browser demuxes. What we have today. The browser keeps
    them in sync perfectly because they share one timeline. The only cost is
    that "audio only" needs a second file.
  - Adaptive streaming, the way YouTube and Netflix do it. The recording is
    packaged once into fragmented MP4 segments with audio and video as separate
    representations plus a manifest (HLS or DASH). A player library such as
    hls.js, dash.js or Shaka fetches segments of each track and feeds them into
    Media Source Extensions, which syncs them by timestamp. You get audio-only
    mode, quality switching on bad connections, and no duplicated audio bytes.
    It needs a packaging step at import, a player library in the page, and the
    service worker caching segments instead of one file. About two days of work.

  My recommendation. Not worth the third option for an internal tool yet: our
  videos are already only 100 to 300 MB per hour, and the offline duplication of
  audio inside a video pin is about 30 MB per hour. Do the always-on extract at
  import now. Revisit adaptive streaming only if people start pinning video
  routinely or watching over hotel wifi becomes a complaint.

⏺ Yes, this is very likely the cause, and the evidence is clear.

  Every Google Meet recording on the server has its index at the end of the 
  file. An mp4 needs its moov atom (the table of where each frame lives) before
  the browser can play anything. Google writes it last. So on every play the
  browser fetches the head of the file, finds no index, then fetches the tail,
  then finally starts, and every seek repeats a round trip through the server.
  On a laptop on office wifi that is a blink. On a phone with a bad signal it is
  exactly the "I press play and nothing happens" you see. Teams recordings are
  written index-first and my audio-only extracts are too, which is why those
  feel fine.

  ┌─────────────────────────────────┬────────────────┐
  │          Files checked          │ Index position │
  ├─────────────────────────────────┼────────────────┤
  │ 11 of 12 newest Meet recordings │ at the end     │

  ├─────────────────────────────────┼────────────────┤
  │ Audio-only extracts             │ at the start   │
  └─────────────────────────────────┴────────────────┘

  streams the whole video file, so a phone pulls 100 to 500 kbps of pixels it

     the video file, and the offline pin becomes instant.

  problems you are hitting. Say the word and I'll run both as the next workflow,

⏺ Not today. Offline mode is read-only and records nothing, so anything you do
  while offline leaves no trace on the server.

  What is lost right now when you read offline

  - The "viewed" event. Online, opening a meeting hits the transcript API, which
    logs a view for the activity bar and bumps last accessed. Offline, the
    service worker answers from cache and the server never hears about it.
  - Playback position and which sections you read. Those are not tracked even
    online, so nothing changes there.
  - Edits, labels, shares, AI actions. All disabled offline by design, so there
    is nothing to replay.

  How it should work. An outbox in the same IndexedDB the pins use. While
  offline, the page appends small events with their original timestamps: view,
  play, seek, maybe "read the summary". On reconnect, the sync loop posts the
  batch to one new endpoint with an idempotency key per event, and the server
  writes them into the existing activity table with the offline timestamps, not
  the upload time. Chrome's Background Sync can trigger the flush even if the
  tab was closed before the connection came back. Roughly a day of work, and it
  stays strictly append-only telemetry, so no conflict handling.

  What I would keep out of it. Edits to titles, notes, or speaker names made
  offline. That needs conflict resolution against changes colleagues made
  meanwhile, and the value is low compared with the risk of silently overwriting
  someone's work. Read-only offline is the right call for now.
