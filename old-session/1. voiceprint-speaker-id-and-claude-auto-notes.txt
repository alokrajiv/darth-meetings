⏺ Yes — this is very buildable, and your app already collects everything needed.
  Here's the assessment.

  What you have today: AssemblyAI's diarization gives per-file labels only (A,
  B, C — no memory across files; they have no voice-enrollment API). But you
  keep a local copy of every audio file, you have per-utterance timestamps, and
  every time someone renames "Speaker A" → a real person in speaker_mappings,
  that's a free, human-verified voice sample of that person. That's the
  enrollment data.

  How auto-detection would work (speaker embeddings / "voiceprints"):

  1. Enroll from past corrections. For each speaker a user has already named,
  slice a few of their longest clean utterances out of the stored audio with
  ffmpeg, run them through a speaker-embedding model, and average into one
  vector per person. Good CPU-friendly options: SpeechBrain's ECAPA-TDNN
  (speechbrain/spkrec-ecapa-voxceleb, 192-dim) or pyannote's embedding model.
  Both run fine on the .6 VM's CPU — an embedding takes well under a second per
  clip.
  2. Match on new uploads. When a new transcript finishes, for each diarized
  speaker take their 3–5 longest utterances, embed them, and cosine-compare
  against the stored voiceprints. Above a threshold → pre-fill the speaker name
  as a suggestion (shown as "detected — confirm?", not silently applied); below
  → leave as A/B like today.
  3. Self-improving loop. Every confirmation or correction updates that person's
  voiceprint (rolling average of embeddings), so accuracy improves with each
  meeting.

  Storage: a voiceprints table keyed to your existing people table — the vector
  fits in a float4[] or jsonb; pgvector is nice-to-have but overkill at your
  scale (a linear scan over even hundreds of people is instant).

  Deployment shape: a small Python sidecar on .6 (its own venv, per your
  convention) exposing one endpoint — POST /embed with an audio path + segment
  list, returns vectors. The Next.js post-transcription step calls it, does the
  cosine math, and writes suggestions into speaker_mappings.

  Caveats worth knowing upfront:
  - Accuracy depends on diarization quality — if AAI merges two people into one
  label, the embedding is polluted. Picking long, non-overlapping utterances
  mitigates this.
  - Meeting audio (compressed, far-field mics) is harsher than the models'
  training data; expect ~90%+ on regular attendees with a tuned threshold, not
  perfection. The threshold needs empirical tuning on your own meetings.
  - pyannote's models need a HuggingFace gated-access accept; SpeechBrain's
  ECAPA doesn't — I'd start with SpeechBrain.

  A cheap complementary trick: have an LLM scan the transcript for
  self/cross-identification ("Thanks, Germaine", "this is Alok from…") and merge
  those hints with the voice match — the two signals combined get you close to
  fully automatic naming.

  If you want it, the natural first milestone is: backfill voiceprints from all
  already-named speakers, then wire suggestions into the next upload. Say the
  word and I'll build it.
